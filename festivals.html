Eid-ul-Fitr

Celebrated after Ramadan with prayers, feasts and joy!

Eid-ul-Fitr – Festival of Joy

Eid-ul-Fitr is one of the most important and joyful festivals celebrated in Pakistan and by Muslims all over the world. It marks the end of the holy month of Ramadan, a time of fasting, prayer, and self-reflection. Eid begins with a special prayer at mosques and open grounds, where people come together dressed in new clothes to thank Allah for the strength and patience during Ramadan.

The day is filled with happiness, family gatherings, and lots of delicious food. People greet each other with “Eid Mubarak” and exchange warm hugs. Elders give Eidi (money or gifts) to children, which makes the celebration even more exciting for the younger ones.

A variety of traditional dishes are prepared, especially sweet treats like Sheer Khurma – a dessert made with milk, vermicelli, dates, and nuts. Families visit relatives, friends, and neighbors to share meals and spread joy. Many people also give charity to the poor so that everyone can enjoy the festival equally.

Eid-ul-Fitr is not just a religious festival — it’s a celebration of love, unity, and thankfulness. It brings people closer and reminds us to care for one another.

Independence Day

14th August — fireworks, flags, and national pride.

Independence Day – 14th August

Independence Day is celebrated every year on 14th August to mark the day Pakistan became an independent nation in 1947. It is a day of great pride and honor for every Pakistani, as it reminds us of the sacrifices made by our ancestors during the freedom movement. The day began with the efforts of leaders like Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah, who dreamed of a separate homeland for Muslims of the subcontinent.

On this day, the national flag is raised at schools, homes, and government buildings. The day starts with a flag hoisting ceremony, national anthem, and prayers for peace and progress. Buildings and streets are decorated with green and white lights, and the air is filled with patriotic songs and energy.

People dress in green and white clothes, children wave flags, and many take part in parades and special programs that highlight Pakistan's history and culture. Fireworks and air shows by the armed forces are also part of the celebrations in major cities.

Independence Day is not just about celebration — it's a reminder to stay united, respect our freedom, and build a better future for Pakistan. It fills every heart with love for the country and a promise to always protect and serve it.

Basant

Punjab's colorful kite flying festival with music and food.

Basant – The Festival of Kites

Basant is one of the most colorful and exciting traditional festivals celebrated mainly in Lahore and parts of Punjab. It marks the beginning of the spring season, usually in late February or early March. The sky becomes a battlefield of vibrant kites, with people on rooftops competing, laughing, and cheering every time a rival kite goes down.

The festival is all about fun, color, and joy. People wear yellow clothes to represent the freshness of spring, and the whole city comes alive with music, food, and laughter. Rooftops are filled with families and friends enjoying traditional snacks, BBQs, and of course, non-stop kite flying.

At night, the celebration continues with lights, music, and fireworks. People decorate their homes and rooftops with fairy lights and enjoy dance parties and dhol beats that echo across the city.

Even though Basant has been restricted in some areas due to safety concerns, it remains one of the most loved cultural festivals of Pakistan. It shows how lively and joyful the spirit of the people truly is.
