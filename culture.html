Languages

Pakistan is home to Urdu, Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto, Balochi and more! Pakistan is a country rich in languages, with over 70 spoken across its regions. The national language is Urdu, which acts as a unifying language and is widely understood and spoken, even though it's not the native tongue of most people. Urdu is poetic, expressive, and deeply tied to the culture and history of the region. Alongside Urdu, English is also an official language and is commonly used in education, government, and business. Every province in Pakistan has its own beautiful language. In Punjab, people mostly speak Punjabi, which is warm, vibrant, and full of folk songs and stories. In Sindh, the local language is Sindhi, which has a rich literary history and a distinct script. Pashto is widely spoken in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and parts of Balochistan, known for its strength and depth in poetry and tradition. In Balochistan, people also speak Balochi, a powerful language with strong tribal roots. Another widely spoken language is Saraiki, mostly used in Southern Punjab. It has its own unique identity and shares some similarities with Punjabi. In Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir, you’ll hear languages like Shina, Balti, Burushaski, and Kashmiri — each reflecting the culture of the mountainous north. All these languages make Pakistan culturally rich and diverse. While Urdu connects everyone, local languages give color and character to each region, making Pakistan a beautiful mix of voices and traditions.

National Dress of Pakistan – Shalwar Kameez

The shalwar kameez is the national dress of Pakistan, proudly worn by both men and women across the country. It’s a comfortable, stylish, and traditional outfit that reflects Pakistan’s cultural values and heritage. The kameez is a long tunic or shirt, while the shalwar is a loose-fitting trouser that tapers at the ankles. Together, they make a perfect outfit for Pakistan’s climate — cool in summer and warm in winter.

Men usually wear plain or lightly patterned shalwar kameez in cotton, wash-and-wear, or khaddar fabrics, often paired with waistcoats for formal events. Women’s shalwar kameez comes in a wide range of colors, embroidery styles, and fabrics like chiffon, lawn, and silk. Women also wear a dupatta (a long scarf) with it, which adds grace and tradition to the outfit.

Whether it’s a casual day, a school uniform, or a festive wedding, the shalwar kameez fits every occasion. It represents unity in diversity, as each region adds its own twist — like Sindhi mirror work, Punjabi phulkaris, or Balochi embroidery. It’s more than just clothing — it’s a symbol of Pakistani identity and pride.

Food

Enjoy spicy dishes like biryani, karahi, nihari and halwa puri.

Traditional Foods of Pakistan

Food is a big part of Pakistani culture, and every region of the country brings its own delicious flavors to the table. Pakistani cuisine is famous for its strong spices, rich taste, and variety of dishes that reflect the diversity of the people. From street food to wedding meals, Pakistani food is full of life and flavor.

One of the most loved dishes is Biryani – a spicy rice dish made with layers of basmati rice, meat (chicken, beef, or mutton), and aromatic spices like cardamom, cloves, and saffron. It is especially popular in Karachi and Lahore and is a must-have at parties and celebrations.

Another famous dish is Nihari, a slow-cooked meat curry that's rich and full of flavor. Traditionally eaten for breakfast with naan, it's especially popular in winter and is loved for its deep, warm spices.

For a traditional breakfast treat, Halwa Puri is a top favorite. It includes crispy, deep-fried puris (flatbread) served with sweet suji halwa and spicy chana (chickpeas). It’s often eaten on weekends or special occasions and is commonly found in breakfast restaurants across the country.

Pakistani food is more than just meals – it’s a celebration of taste, culture, and togetherness. Whether it’s a street vendor or a home-cooked dish, the food of Pakistan is loved not just by locals, but by people all over the world.
